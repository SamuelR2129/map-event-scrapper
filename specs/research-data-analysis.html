9. Most-featured acts: live acts vs DJs

There is no artist-type field in the data, so this split is derived: each billed name is classified by the genres it appears under (electronic/techno/house/DJ/vinyl → DJ; rock/punk/jazz/ acoustic/indie → live act), with manual overrides where the genre mix is misleading. Names that are programme titles (Wharf Life, Open Decks, BYO Vinyl Night, Jazz Night, Bernies theme nights) are excluded; Jazz Night: <act> is credited to the act after the colon.

Breeze and Bellicanta's are excluded from the ranking. 23 of Breeze's 31 bills come from the recurring event title Jazz Night: Breeze, and Bellicanta's 16 bills are a single weekly solo residency at Theydy's. Both counts measure a residency listing rather than band bookings. Breeze's remaining 8 standalone jazz bills fall outside the top 10; Bellicanta's has no bills outside the residency.

Findings

  • Rhodes is the most-featured live act — 21 bills across 8 venues and 19 weeks. Gauze (12 bills across 9 venues) and Charlie Farmer (12 across 9) are similarly itinerant, the signature of a support-circuit band rather than a resident.
  • Volume is not the same as headlining. Bardvarkk reaches 14 bills and Trophy Wyfe 11, while Portrait Of A Lady's 10 bills span 5 venues. None is a single-room residency.

  • The biggest DJ programming is event-branded, not artist-branded. Wharf Life (38 weeks) and Open Decks (36) are excluded as programme titles; no named DJ comes close to those runs. DJ culture here is a night, not a roster.
  • Rose White is the one material omission: 16 bills across 8 venues, mixing electronic, punk and alt line-ups. There is no defensible way to call them band or DJ from this data, so they are reported as unclassified rather than forced into a bucket.

13. Promoters

Basis: researchData/events.json, the single merged file produced from every events-*.json dump. Duplicates are collapsed by id, then by (eventDateTime, eventName), then clustered by venue so spelling drift and leaked promoter prefixes do not create separate events (Grindhouse - Croatian Club Side Room / Croatian Club / Croatian Wickham Sports Club are one row; so are The Oak / The Oak Hotel and Ori Hotel / The Oriental Hotel). A final pass collapses the same bill on the same night at the same venue when the names differ only by a truncated tail or one misspelled word; it also folds the same bill at the same venue a day or two apart, and a one-off bill that appears exactly twice a week apart, while leaving weekly residencies alone. Result: 1,388 events, of which 166 carry an eventPromoters value across 111 distinct strings (123 distinct names after splitting multi-promoter rows on commas). Before the 2026-11-14 poster pass only 4 rows had a promoter; the June 2026 batch added 5 more.

New Annual (6) and Great Southern Nights (11) lead the raw counts but are excluded from the table: they are festival/council program brands rather than independent promoters. Full Send Friday (3) is excluded for the same reason — it is Hamilton Station's weekly heavy night, not an outside promoter. Removing them leaves the independent promoters with 3+ events:

Findings

  • Promoter disclosure is the exception, not the rule: 166 of 1,388 events (12.0%). Most posters do not name a promoter at all, so these counts are a floor, not a ranking of who runs the most gigs.
  • Concentration is low. The largest independent promoter has 10 events across ten months, and only after folding Kova into Kova Events; the next is Bad Behaviour with 6. This is a venue-led scene, consistent with §3 (five venues = half of all listings), not a promoter-led one.
  • Kova and Kova Events are the same operator. The canonical alias lives in the applier and the merge script; C.T and C.T. are folded to C.T. (4 events).

What this analysis supports

Supported:

  • Share of listings by day tab, venue, genre and price bracket.
  • Venue concentration and relative intensity (listings per active week).
  • Venue-level programming identity (genre specialisation, free vs paid character).
  • Price distribution among known prices, and the free/paid/unknown split.
  • Residency structure and the size of the one-off layer.
  • Billing depth and the long-tail/repeat split among bill entries.
  • A live-act vs DJ split, with the caveat that it is inferred from genre and manually overridden for ambiguous names (Rose White, 16 bills, is left unclassified).

Not supported:

  • Absolute event counts ("1,388 events happened") — the unit is a listing, not an event.
  • Any calendar claim ("what's on this Friday") — the timestamp is the day tab and save week.
  • Month-over-month trends — two weeks inside the range are missing (W18 and W39), and W43–W45 are only partially covered.
  • Geography, distance or neighbourhood analysis — coordinates are present but not used as reliable location data here.
  • Artist identity or "top artist" claims — eventName mixes artists and named nights, with no ID. The bands-vs-DJs ranking in section 9 is the closest defensible reading, and it is heuristic.
  • Weekly capacity or attendance — the data records advertising, not ticket sales.

Summary

  1. The usable unit is a listing, not a row. The merged corpus now holds 1,388 events from 286 images, which equal 1,388 listings at (bill, venue, ISO week).
  2. It is a Thursday–Sunday scene. 90% of listings sit on those four days; Saturday and Friday are tied (394 vs 372), and Sunday is a strong third (297) rather than a quiet night.
  3. Five venues are half the scene. Lass O'Gowrie (202), Hamilton Station (182), Maru Hi-Fi (142), The Oak (110) and King Street (91) produce 52.7% of listings; 5 venues reach 50% and 12 reach 80%, with the top ten accounting for 75.6%.
  4. Genre is a property of the room, not the city. Bernies is 100% dance/LGBTQIA+, The Underground 100% jazz, The Oak 58% vinyl, The Beauford 74% rock. Only indie+rock and punk+rock appear across many venues.
  5. Nearly two-thirds of listings are free (63.5%). Where money is charged, it concentrates in King Street ($47 median), Stag & Hunter ($34), Hamilton Station ($25) and Lass O'Gowrie ($25). Vinyl (97.2% free) and acoustic (83.5%) are the free end; punk (52.5%) and rock (57.3%) the paid end.
  6. Bills are short and names are mostly one-off. Half of all listings are single-act (714), and 75.8% of the 1,922 raw billed strings appear exactly once; only 19 appear 10+ times.
  7. A small permanent core carries the scene. 34 residencies cover 23.3% of listings, led by Wharf Life (38 weeks), BYO Vinyl Night (37) and Open Decks (36); the other 990 bill+venue pairs appear once.
  8. The data is honest about its gaps. Two weeks inside the range are missing (W18 and W39), W43–W45 are only partially covered, 10.6% of prices are unparseable, and W38 is a catch-up spike. June 2026 (W23–W26) is no longer a gap: it was recovered from 20 retrospective posters whose dates live in their file creation dates — so this still supports a portrait of the scene, not a time series, and not any geographic or calendar-specific claim.
  9. The most-featured live act is Rhodes (21 bills across 8 venues and 19 weeks), followed by S.M.D (16) and Bardvarkk (14). Breeze and Bellicanta's were excluded because their counts come from residency listings (Jazz Night: Breeze and the weekly Bellicanta's show), not band bookings.
  10. Beam and DJ Qyto top the DJ standings at 9 bills each. The Oak's vinyl selectors — PC Dysco (12 appearances), Rick A Mortis (9) and Black Tiger (7) — are excluded from the DJ ranking. No named DJ exceeds 9 bills because the biggest dance programming is event-branded (Wharf Life, Open Decks).
  11. Excluding FREE and DANCE, ROCK is the leading genre — 274 listings (19.7%), and still first among paid listings at 67 (18.6%); PUNK is second on paid listings (47).
  12. WITCHHOUSE SELECT is a brand tag, not a genre. It appears in 41 raw rows / 41 listings (3.0%), most often at Lass O'Gowrie Hotel (10); DJ Gunggek leads the tagged bills with 5, then Kev Sev with 4.
  13. Heavy music is concentrated at Hamilton Station. 45 of 80 metal/heavy/hardcore listings (56%) are there; Disco leads the artists with 7 venue-weeks and Fat Monica has 6, while 81.2% of the 224 acts appear only once.
  14. Promoters are disclosed on only 12.0% of events. The corpus holds 1,388 events, of which 166 carry a promoter; excluding the New Annual and Great Southern Nights program brands, Kova Events leads with 10 (after folding in the Kova variant), then Bad Behaviour with 6, Odyssey with 5, and C.T. with 4 after folding C.T / C.T. together (§13).
