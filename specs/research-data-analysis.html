W23–W26 is the whole of June. There are no source images for June either, so this is a capture gap, not a collapse in the scene. W18 (early May) and W39 (late September) are also missing. W38 at 64 listings is a catch-up batch (about 2× median), not a festival week.

W43–W45 are thin rather than empty: the late re-extraction recovered 4, 6 and 1 listings for those weeks. W46 is the 11 Nov drop. Treat those tail weeks as partial coverage, not a real decline.

Implication: any month-over-month or "scene growth" trend from this data is an artefact. Weekly volume is usable only to describe poster coverage.

9. Most-featured acts: live acts vs DJs

There is no artist-type field in the data, so this split is derived: each billed name is classified by the genres it appears under (electronic/techno/house/DJ/vinyl → DJ; rock/punk/jazz/ acoustic/indie → live act), with manual overrides where the genre mix is misleading. Names that are programme titles (Wharf Life, Open Decks, BYO Vinyl Night, Jazz Night, Bernies theme nights) are excluded; Jazz Night: <act> is credited to the act after the colon.

Breeze and Bellicanta's are excluded from the ranking. 19 of Breeze's 26 bills come from the recurring event title Jazz Night: Breeze, and Bellicanta's 14 bills are a single weekly solo residency at Theydy's. Both counts measure a residency listing rather than band bookings. Breeze's remaining 7 standalone jazz bills fall outside the top 10; Bellicanta's has no bills outside the residency.

Findings

  • Rhodes is the most-featured live act — 20 bills across 8 venues and 18 weeks. Gauze (9 bills across 8 venues) and Charlie Farmer (11 across 8) are similarly itinerant, the signature of a support-circuit band rather than a resident.
  • Volume is not the same as headlining. Bardvarkk and Trophy Wyfe reach 13 and 11 bills but appear entirely in support slots (0 solo bills), while Adam Miller's 9 bills are all solo sets.

  • Rose White is the one material omission: 16 bills across 8 venues, mixing electronic, punk and alt line-ups. There is no defensible way to call them band or DJ from this data, so they are reported as unclassified rather than forced into a bucket.

13. Promoters

Basis: researchData/events.json, the single merged file produced from every events-*.json dump. Duplicates are collapsed by id, then by (eventDateTime, eventName), then clustered by venue so spelling drift and leaked promoter prefixes do not create separate events (Grindhouse - Croatian Club Side Room / Croatian Club / Croatian Wickham Sports Club are one row; so are The Oak / The Oak Hotel and Ori Hotel / The Oriental Hotel). A final pass collapses the same bill on the same night at the same venue when the names differ only by a truncated tail or one misspelled word; it also folds the same bill at the same venue a day or two apart, and a one-off bill that appears exactly twice a week apart, while leaving weekly residencies alone. Result: 1,245 events, of which 161 carry an eventPromoters value across 121 distinct strings (splitting multi-promoter rows on commas). Before the 2026-11-14 poster pass only 4 rows had a promoter.

New Annual (6) and Great Southern Nights (11) lead the raw counts but are excluded from the table: they are festival/council program brands rather than independent promoters. Removing them leaves:

Findings

  • Promoter disclosure is the exception, not the rule: 161 of 1,245 events (12.9%). Most posters do not name a promoter at all, so these counts are a floor, not a ranking of who runs the most gigs.
  • Concentration is low. The largest independent promoter has 10 events across ten months, and only after folding Kova into Kova Events; the next is 5. This is a venue-led scene, consistent with §3 (five venues = half of all listings), not a promoter-led one.
  • Kova and Kova Events are the same operator. The canonical alias lives in the applier and the merge script; C.T and C.T. are folded to C.T. (4 events).
  • De-duplication moves the promoter counts. Collapsing the three 16 Oct Grindhouse rows removed two duplicate credits, and merging the three Dr Sure's Unusual Practice rows did the same for New Brain Communications (4 → 3).
  • Some values are venues or event brands rather than promoters (The Ori, Maru Hi-Fi, Full Send Friday), so read the list as "who is credited on the poster".

  • Venue concentration and relative intensity (listings per active week).
  • Venue-level programming identity (genre specialisation, free vs paid character).
  • Price distribution among known prices, and the free/paid/unknown split.
  • Residency structure and the size of the one-off layer.
  • Billing depth and the long-tail/repeat split among bill entries.
  • A live-act vs DJ split, with the caveat that it is inferred from genre and manually overridden for ambiguous names (Rose White, 16 bills, is left unclassified).

Not supported:

  • Absolute event counts ("1,245 events happened") — the unit is a listing, not an event.
  • Any calendar claim ("what's on this Friday") — the timestamp is the day tab and save week.
  • Month-over-month trends — six weeks are missing, including all of June plus W18 and W39, and W43–W45 are only partially covered.
  • Geography, distance or neighbourhood analysis — coordinates are present but not used as reliable location data here.
  • Artist identity or "top artist" claims — eventName mixes artists and named nights, with no ID. The bands-vs-DJs ranking in section 9 is the closest defensible reading, and it is heuristic.
  • Weekly capacity or attendance — the data records advertising, not ticket sales.

Summary

  1. The usable unit is a listing, not a row. The four dumps held 1,941 rows; merging and de-duplication left 1,245 events, which equal 1,245 listings at (bill, venue, ISO week) — 35.9% of the raw rows were duplicate or near-duplicate.
  2. It is a Thursday–Sunday scene. 91% of listings sit on those four days; Saturday and Friday are tied (355 vs 339), and Sunday is a strong third (262) rather than a quiet night.
  3. Five venues are half the scene. Lass O'Gowrie (182), Hamilton Station (165), Maru Hi-Fi (127), The Oak (98) and King Street (81) produce 52.8% of listings; 5 venues reach 50% and 12 reach 80%, with the top ten accounting for 76.5%.
  4. Genre is a property of the room, not the city. Bernies is 100% dance/LGBTQIA+, The Underground 100% jazz, The Oak 57% vinyl, The Beauford 73% rock. Only indie+rock and punk+rock appear across many venues.
  5. Nearly two-thirds of listings are free (63%). Where money is charged, it concentrates in King Street ($45 median), Stag & Hunter ($34), Hamilton Station ($25) and Lass O'Gowrie ($25). Vinyl and acoustic are the free end; punk and rock the paid end.
  6. Bills are short and names are mostly one-off. Half of all listings are single-act, and 76.0% of the 1,783 raw billed strings appear exactly once; only 15 appear 10+ times.
  7. A small permanent core carries the scene. 27 residencies cover 22.0% of listings, led by Wharf Life (35 weeks), BYO Vinyl Night (34) and Open Decks (32); the other 897 bill+venue pairs appear once.
  8. The data is honest about its gaps. Six weeks are missing (all of June plus W18 and W39), W43–W45 are only partially covered, 10.3% of prices are unparseable, and W38 is a catch-up spike — so this supports a portrait of the scene, not a time series, and not any geographic or calendar-specific claim.
  9. The most-featured live act is Rhodes (20 bills across 8 venues and 18 weeks), followed by S.M.D (16) and Bardvarkk (13). Breeze and Bellicanta's were excluded because their counts come from residency listings (Jazz Night: Breeze and the weekly Bellicanta's show), not band bookings.
  10. The most-featured DJ is PC Dysco (11 bills, all at The Oak), followed by Beam, DJ Qyto and Rick A Mortis (9 each). No named DJ exceeds 11 bills because the biggest dance programming is event-branded (Wharf Life, Open Decks).
  11. Excluding FREE and DANCE, ROCK is the leading genre — 238 listings (19.1%), and still first among paid listings at 60 (18.0%); PUNK is second on paid listings (43).
  12. WITCHHOUSE SELECT is a brand tag, not a genre. It appears in 37 raw rows / 37 listings (3.0%), most often at Lass O'Gowrie Hotel (10); DJ Gunggek leads the tagged bills with 5, then Kev Sev with 4.
  13. Heavy music is concentrated at Hamilton Station. 41 of 72 metal/heavy/hardcore listings (57%) are there; Disco leads the artists with 7 venue-weeks and Fat Monica has 6, while 83.5% of the 206 acts appear only once.
  14. Promoters are disclosed on only 12.9% of events. After merging and de-duplicating the four dumps into 1,245 events (the per-date dumps were then removed), 161 carry a promoter; excluding the New Annual and Great Southern Nights program brands, Kova Events leads with 10 (after folding in the Kova variant), then Odyssey and Bad Behaviour with 5 each, and C.T. with 4 after folding C.T / C.T. together (§13).
